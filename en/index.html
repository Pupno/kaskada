01 Executive summary

Industrial heat from digital infrastructure, delivered as a public service.

Kaskáda is a modular data centre designed from the ground up so that its waste heat enters the district heating network instead of the atmosphere.

Slovak district heating operators face a coal phase-out and rising ETS prices. At the same time, European demand for sovereign, taxonomy-aligned compute is growing. Kaskáda answers both with one asset: a 10 MW colocation and cloud facility with 100 % liquid cooling, whose waste heat is lifted by CO₂ heat pumps to 85 to 95 °C, the supply temperature of most Slovak primary hot-water networks.

The project is structured as a public-private partnership with the municipal heating utility. Phase 1 CAPEX is €95 M with a grant share of 15 % (conservative: thermal part only) to 40 % (ambitious: plus the EU Innovation Fund). Project IRR ≈ 11.7 % with the full grant, ≈ 8.5 % conservative, ≈ 7.3 % without grants; private-capital payback ≈ 7.6 years. All figures come from the open 25-year model, downloadable as Excel.

Why nowThe recast Energy Efficiency Directive (EU) 2023/1791 requires data centres above 500 kW to report energy KPIs (Art. 12) and those above 1 MW to assess and, where feasible, implement waste-heat recovery (Art. 26). Slovak district heating is predominantly gas-fired: the regulated variable heat price averaged €93.4/MWh in 2025 (ÚRSO), while recovered data-centre heat can be delivered for roughly €52/MWh (electricity ÷ (COP − 1) plus heat-pump capital). The state-owned MH Teplárenský Holding serves ~320,000 households in six cities, so the heat buyer is already the state. And the Modernisation Fund must spend its Slovak envelope by 2030.
Status, honestlyWhat exists: this proposal, the model, the data. What does not: a site, a licence, an anchor tenant, a signed heat offtake. The plan therefore opens with a 300 kW wedge pilot at a municipal pool (Deep Green model, under €0.5 M, no ÚRSO licence, revenue within 12 months) rather than a €95 M build.
Why it is openThe full proposal, data, model and sources are public under CC BY 4.0. Any utility, city or ministry can adopt it, change the assumptions in the calculator and apply it to its own site. The goal is not one project but a national programme.
02 Concept

Liquid cooling as an industrial heat source.

Direct-to-chip and immersion cooling raise the coolant outlet temperature to 55 to 65 °C. A two-stage CO₂ (R744) heat pump with COP ≈ 3.4 lifts it to 85 to 95 °C. Three 500 m³ storage tanks buffer eight hours of output. The utility keeps its own plants as backup; Kaskáda supplies baseload heat 8,760 hours a year, because IT load has no heating season. Reference plant: Infomaniak D4 in Geneva (since November 2024) returns ~95 % of its energy to the SIG network at 67 °C, 1.7 MW thermal, ~6,000 homes, with the offtake contracted before construction.

Sequencing matters: cloud revenue first, heat second. Heat is 10 to 15 % of revenue, a margin enhancer, a permitting asset and the key to grants, not the core business. Grants fund the thermal part only, never servers or white space; the compute business must service its own capital.

03 Site

Žilina first, Košice second, eight cities in the programme.

Eight Slovak cities with district heating above 250 GWh/yr were scored on six weighted criteria: heat demand, decarbonisation pressure, 110 kV grid access, fibre connectivity, land, and municipal will. Žilina scores highest on decarbonisation pressure and land; Košice has the largest network and strongest grid. Full scores and map: Lokality (Slovak, data in data/locations.json).

07 Ask to the state

Eight measures that turn one project into a national programme.

  1. Recognise data-centre waste heat as a low-carbon source in the Heat Energy Act and in the efficient district heating definition (EED Art. 26).
  2. A dedicated Modernisation Fund call for integrating industrial and data-centre waste heat into district heating.
  3. A regulatory framework (ÚRSO) for 15-year indexed heat purchase agreements from waste heat.
  4. A single point of contact for DC + DH projects across ministries and the regulator.
  5. Public administration as anchor tenant: 20 to 30 % of capacity for government cloud.
  6. Priority grid connection and accelerated permitting for taxonomy-aligned projects.
  7. An InvestEU guarantee window for the first three projects.
  8. A NECP target: 300 GWh/yr of heat from data centres and industry into district heating by 2035.
  9. A pilot programme for municipal pools and aquaparks: sub-500 kW compute containers with a heat exchanger into the hot-water loop, no licence, results within one season.

Kill criteria, stated up front

  1. MHTH and two municipal utilities decline a heat purchase agreement above €45/MWh: the Slovak premium is theoretical and we do not build.
  2. No site with grid, network and fibre after screening the six MHTH cities and the ten largest municipal systems.
  3. Fewer than 1 MW of compute LOIs after six months of pre-selling.
  4. State-aid rules exclude a private data-centre operator and no municipal JV fixes it.
  5. The wedge pilot cannot beat the pool's gas cost by at least 20 % after a metered season.

We do not ask for electricity subsidies, ETS exemptions, state ownership or capacity guarantees. Full policy brief with objections answered: Pre štát (Slovak).
